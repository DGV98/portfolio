# Resisting the Easy Button

>> My path into software engineering has not been linear. I graduated from college
as a Molecular Engineering major, a degree that the university was admittedly 
"testing out". I like to think of it as a glorified physics degree, as the bulk of 
my coursework was in the physics department. I enjoyed my time solving rigorous problems, 
as most of my problem sets involved thinking very hard for many hours to come up with a
solution. Trying things and failing, and trying all over again. I found that this was what I enjoyed
most about what I chose to study, but I quickly came to the realization that I would have to make
money in the outside world. I decided to pivot, somewhere in my third year. I started taking
CS classes, which allowed me to continue thinking and solving hard problems that 
scratched the itch in my brain, the part that wanted to *think*. 

>> I eventually found my first role in a consulting company as a software engineer and was able
to learn about cloud computing, although this role did not provide me with a lot of experience 
with clients to consider myself a fleshed out engineer.
